\section*{1. System Implemented}

The pipeline proposed in Section~5 of the project proposal has been
implemented in full. Raw KITTI Velodyne scans are preprocessed (NaN/Inf
removal, 60~m range gating, 0.15~m voxel-grid downsampling) and corrupted by
a physics-based weather model: extinction follows an empirical Mie fit
$\alpha = 0.003R^{0.6}$~m$^{-1}$ for rain rate $R$ (mm/h) at 905~nm, after
LISA [Kilic et al., arXiv:2107.07004]. Each return is attenuated by the
two-way transmittance $e^{-2\alpha r}$; a droplet intercepting the beam
relocates the return to its own (nearer) range, weakly attenuated echoes
fall below the detection threshold and are \emph{deleted}, and survivors
receive SNR-dependent range jitter. Gaussian sensor jitter
($\sigma=0.05$~m) and uniform outliers (8\%) are applied on top, and three
severity presets (10/25/50~mm/h) follow the Robo3D convention
[Kong et al., ICCV 2023].

The deletion channel matters for how every result below should be read: a
denoiser can remove spurious points and reposition jittered ones, but it
cannot restore a return the weather destroyed. Reconstruction error
therefore has a strictly positive floor, quantified in Section~2.

Six denoisers are evaluated. Three require no training: (a) the statistical
baseline chaining Statistical Outlier Removal ($k=20$, $\alpha=2.0$) and
Radius Outlier Removal (4 neighbours within 0.6~m); (b) IDSOR
[arXiv:2602.05876], which normalises the $k$-NN distance statistic by range
so far-field structure is not eroded and then tightens the threshold for
weak returns; and (c) a temporal-consistency filter keeping only points with
support in the previous sweep, preceded by ICP ego-motion compensation.
Three are learned: (d) a PointNet autoencoder (0.83M parameters, Chamfer
loss), (e) a lightweight DGCNN (0.22M parameters) regressing per-point
displacements and classifying injected noise, and (f) \emph{RangeRestoreNet},
a dual-head U-Net over a $64\times2048$ range image (1.94M parameters)
developed here, which classifies noise per pixel and predicts a bounded
per-pixel range correction.

Intensity is carried throughout. Weather backscatter returns markedly less
energy than a solid surface at the same range --- measured here, real
returns have median intensity 0.150 against 0.051 for injected noise --- and
methods (b) and (f) exploit that; the earlier geometry-only pipeline
discarded the channel entirely.

\textbf{Data.} 262 scans from two public KITTI raw drives
(2011\_09\_26, drives 0001 and 0005), split 213/26/28 into
train/validation/test by frame. The official KITTI 3D-object Velodyne
archive (29~GB, login-gated) was deliberately not used: the raw split
provides identical sensor data at 490~MB, sufficient for denoising
experiments. Training used 28{,}510 patches of 256 points with 3{,}415
held-out validation patches; on team hardware (Apple M1, MPS backend) both
models were trained for 10 epochs, and the checkpoint retained is the one
minimising \emph{validation} loss rather than the final epoch.

\section*{2. Reconstruction Quality (Proposal Table-1 axes)}

Mean $\pm$ standard deviation over 10 held-out frames $\times$ 3
independent noise draws (30 paired observations, moderate severity,
25~mm/h). CD = Chamfer Distance, HD$_{95}$ = 95th-percentile Hausdorff.
Every pairwise CD difference below is significant at $p < 10^{-5}$
(Wilcoxon signed-rank over the paired observations).

\textbf{Oracle gap} & \textbf{Rem. F1} & \textbf{Reten.}\\
\midrule
Noisy input     & 0.572 $\pm$ 0.111 & ---   & 100\%  & ---   & ---\\
\emph{Oracle (perf. removal)} & \emph{0.104 $\pm$ 0.003} & \emph{0.353} & \emph{0\%} & \emph{1.000} & \emph{68.9\%}\\
DGCNN           & \textbf{0.111 $\pm$ 0.008} & \textbf{0.342} & \textbf{1.6\%} & 0.788 & 78.7\%\\
IDSOR           & 0.174 $\pm$ 0.013 & 0.827 & 15.0\% & 0.665 & 81.3\%\\
RangeRestoreNet & 0.215 $\pm$ 0.035 & 0.459 & 23.8\% & \textbf{0.814} & 65.6\%\\
SOR + ROR       & 0.251 $\pm$ 0.020 & 1.529 & 31.5\% & 0.628 & 78.7\%\\
Temporal + SOR  & 0.317 $\pm$ 0.035 & 2.173 & 45.5\% & 0.567 & 81.4\%\\
PointNet AE     & 0.521 $\pm$ 0.092 & 3.353 & 89.1\% & 0.000 & 100.0\%\\
\bottomrule
\end{tabular}
\end{table}

The \emph{oracle gap} column is the fraction of the \emph{recoverable}
error a method leaves behind,
$(\mathrm{CD}_{\text{method}}-\mathrm{CD}_{\text{oracle}})/
(\mathrm{CD}_{\text{noisy}}-\mathrm{CD}_{\text{oracle}})$. It is reported
because a raw Chamfer Distance is uninterpretable when 31\% of returns are
destroyed beyond recovery: 0\% means a method matched a perfect noise
classifier, 100\% that it achieved nothing.

The oracle row is the reference point: it deletes every injected point and
keeps every real one, and still scores CD 0.104 rather than zero, because
the weather model destroys 31\% of returns that no denoiser can restore.
Read against that floor, the DGCNN has closed 98\% of the recoverable gap.

The DGCNN is the strongest method by a wide margin, and marginally exceeds
the oracle on HD$_{95}$ (0.342 vs 0.353) --- a difference of 3\% on a single
tail statistic, which should not be over-read. An earlier revision of this
work attributed it to the DGCNN's displacement head correcting jitter the
oracle leaves untouched; direct measurement (Section~3) shows that head
changes point positions by 2.6\% and alters Chamfer Distance not at all, so
that explanation does not hold.

RangeRestoreNet achieves the best noise-removal F1 of any method (0.814,
above the DGCNN's 0.788) at 40$\times$ lower latency, but a worse Chamfer
Distance, because it is recall-heavy: it finds more of the noise and removes
real returns along with it, retaining only 65.6\% of the cloud --- below even
the oracle's 68.9\%.

The statistical baseline remains competitive and far cheaper (Section~4),
consistent with the literature survey's expectation that classical filters
handle additive outliers well; its weakness is recall (removal F1 0.628,
recall 0.53), as a global distance threshold cannot separate weather
backscatter from sparse genuine structure at range. The temporal filter
under-performs the plain baseline here: requiring support in the previous
sweep also discards legitimately new returns at the leading edge of the
scene.

The autoencoder under-performs on every axis: a global bottleneck
over-smooths fine scan-line structure, and having no removal head it cannot
delete an injected point at all (removal F1 exactly 0). Its retention of
100\% confirms it is a pure displacement model. Section~3 shows this is far
more damaging than its modest CD suggests.

\textbf{Ablations.} Sweeping the DGCNN's outlier threshold over
0.3--0.7 confirms the default of 0.5 sits at the CD optimum (0.113, flat
between 0.5 and 0.6) with removal F1 peaking slightly earlier at 0.4
(0.788); the model is not sensitive to this choice. Sweeping SOR parameters
confirms the proposal's tuning discussion: tighter thresholds
($\alpha=1.0$) improve CD by $\sim$6\% over the default. ICP ego-motion
compensation improves the temporal filter from CD 0.227 to 0.177 while
retaining 5 percentage points more of the cloud, confirming that the
uncompensated variant was deleting static structure rather than
backscatter.

\section*{3. Downstream Detection Impact}

KITTI raw frames carry no ground-truth boxes, so a \emph{detection
consistency} protocol was used: a KITTI-pretrained PointPillars detector
(MMDetection3D, CPU inference) is run on each clean cloud and its boxes
taken as reference; recall/precision of the detections on noisy and denoised
clouds are measured against that reference (BEV centre matching, 1~m
tolerance, score $\geq 0.4$, 10 frames).

Evaluated over all 28 held-out frames $\times$ 3 noise seeds (84 paired
observations). $p$-values are paired Wilcoxon signed-rank against the
perfect-removal oracle.

\begin{table}[h]
\centering\small
\begin{tabular}{lccc}
\toprule
\textbf{Condition} & \textbf{Recall} & \textbf{Precision} & \textbf{$p$ vs oracle}\\
\midrule
Noisy input & 0.259 $\pm$ 0.231 & 0.806 & $<10^{-4}$\\
\emph{Oracle (perfect removal)} & \emph{0.479 $\pm$ 0.325} & \emph{0.805} & ---\\
DGCNN       & \textbf{0.452 $\pm$ 0.298} & 0.764 & 0.153 (n.s.)\\
RangeRestoreNet & 0.403 $\pm$ 0.320 & 0.788 & $2\times10^{-4}$\\
SOR + ROR   & 0.392 $\pm$ 0.303 & 0.737 & $<10^{-4}$\\
Temporal + SOR & 0.389 $\pm$ 0.269 & 0.795 & ---\\
IDSOR       & 0.368 $\pm$ 0.291 & \textbf{0.826} & $<10^{-4}$\\
PointNet AE & 0.062 $\pm$ 0.104 & 0.773 & $<10^{-4}$\\
\bottomrule
\end{tabular}
\end{table}

The weather corruption costs the detector 74\% of its clean-cloud
detections. Every denoiser recovers a significant share ($p<10^{-4}$ against
the noisy baseline) except the autoencoder, which makes matters
significantly worse.

\textbf{Perfect removal is the ceiling.} The DGCNN is the only method
statistically indistinguishable from the oracle; all others fall
significantly short. This sample size matters: an earlier revision of this
work evaluated detection on 10 frames, observed that two methods exceeded
the oracle's mean recall, and drew the opposite conclusion. Per-frame recall
has a standard deviation of $\approx 0.3$ about a mean of $0.4$, so at that
sample size a gap of 0.05 between means is uninformative; at 84 paired
observations the ordering reverses and the apparent effect disappears. The
geometric metrics, whose between-frame variance is an order of magnitude
smaller, did not suffer this problem --- which is precisely why the same
sample size was wrongly assumed adequate for both.

One further result deserves emphasis.

\textbf{Position restoration does not contribute.} Two direct
measurements. The DGCNN's
displacement head changes the mean position error of surviving real points
from 0.0804~m to only 0.0783~m, and removing the displacement entirely
leaves Chamfer Distance unchanged (0.1158 vs 0.1157); a range-restoration
head trained for the same purpose on the range-image model (Section~4)
converged to predicting exactly zero. The reason is intrinsic rather than a
training failure: weather-induced range jitter is zero-mean, so for a single
observation the error-minimising prediction \emph{is} zero, and recovering
it requires a surface prior over neighbouring returns that neither a
per-point patch loss nor a per-pixel image loss supplies. Position
restoration on this corruption is, on the present evidence, not a source of
downstream gain.

\textbf{Reconstruction error does not predict perception quality.} The
autoencoder improves Chamfer Distance by 8.9\% relative to the noisy input
yet destroys the detector, dropping recall from 0.259 to 0.062 --- a 76\%
loss, $p<10^{-4}$, and the only condition that is worse than no denoising at
all. A second, milder inversion points the same way: IDSOR achieves clearly
better Chamfer Distance than SOR+ROR (0.174 vs 0.251) yet slightly lower
detection recall (0.368 vs 0.392, $p=0.069$). Across all six denoisers the
rank correlation between Chamfer Distance and detection recall is only
$\rho=-0.60$ ($p=0.21$) --- directionally correct, statistically weak. The
effect cannot be attributed to point-count inflation, because
after the recombination correction of Section~6 the AE emits exactly 100\%
of the input cardinality; it is the oversmoothing itself that removes the
structure PointPillars relies on. The recombination ablation supplies a
second, independent demonstration: the uncorrected variant achieves a
\emph{better} CD (0.525 vs 0.569) while returning 232\% of the input
points, because densifying a cloud shortens the clean$\rightarrow$denoised
nearest-neighbour term faster than it lengthens the reverse term. Chamfer
Distance is thus improvable both by blurring and by duplication, neither of
which helps perception. This directly substantiates the research gap
identified in Section~3.5 of the proposal, and motivates reporting
retention and a downstream task metric alongside any reconstruction
figure.

\section*{4. Real-Time Feasibility (10 Hz target)}

Per-frame latency was profiled end-to-end on the Apple M1 (batch inference,
patch covering, k-d tree construction included). Dynamic int8 quantisation
was applied to the learning models as the Step-5 optimisation.

\begin{center}
\begin{tabular}{lrrc}
\toprule
\textbf{Method} & \textbf{ms/frame} & \textbf{Hz} & \textbf{$\geq$10~Hz}\\
\midrule
RangeRestoreNet (MPS)        & \textbf{45}    & \textbf{22.1} & \textbf{yes}\\
IDSOR (CPU)                  & \textbf{49}    & \textbf{20.3} & \textbf{yes}\\
SOR+ROR (CPU)                & \textbf{65}    & \textbf{15.5} & \textbf{yes}\\
Temporal+SOR (CPU, incl. ICP)& 278    & 3.6  & no\\
PointNet AE (MPS)            & 1{,}192 & 0.84 & no\\
DGCNN (MPS)                  & 1{,}806 & 0.55 & no\\
DGCNN (CPU int8)             & 11{,}593 & 0.09 & no\\
\bottomrule
\end{tabular}
\end{center}

\textbf{The 10~Hz target is met, and for the first time by a learned
model.} RangeRestoreNet runs end-to-end --- projection, inference and
unprojection --- in 45~ms, a 40$\times$ speedup over the DGCNN. The gain
comes entirely from the representation: a rotating LiDAR is natively a 2-D
array of (laser channel, azimuth) samples, so projecting to a range image
turns neighbourhood queries into array indexing and lets 2-D convolutions
supply the context that EdgeConv had to compute by building a $k$-NN graph
per patch. This is the change the recent literature converged on, and it is
worth roughly two orders of magnitude.

Two caveats keep the classical numbers honest. Part of SOR+ROR's headroom
is supplied by the corruption itself: precipitation destroys 31\% of
returns, so the cloud reaching the filter is smaller than a clear-weather
scan, and the same pipeline is slower in good conditions --- when it is
least needed. And all timings are measured end-to-end from a NumPy array,
including the Open3D conversion that earlier revisions excluded from the
baseline's timer.

The accuracy/latency trade-off is therefore genuine but no longer stark: the
DGCNN buys the lowest Chamfer error (oracle gap 1.6\%) at 40$\times$ the
cost of RangeRestoreNet, which in turn achieves the best noise-removal F1
of any method and recovers more detection recall than either statistical
filter. Dynamic int8 quantisation reduced the AE checkpoint 3.4$\times$
(3.34$\to$0.98~MB), valuable for the proposal's embedded Raspberry~Pi
scenario, but did not improve latency: PyTorch's dynamic backend accelerates
Linear layers, whereas the bottleneck is the $k$-NN gather and patch
assembly.

Dynamic int8 quantisation reduced the AE checkpoint 3.4$\times$
(3.34$\to$0.98~MB), valuable for the proposal's embedded Raspberry~Pi
scenario, but did not improve latency: PyTorch's dynamic backend
accelerates Linear layers, whereas the bottleneck is the k-NN gather and
patch assembly. The measured path to real-time learned denoising is
therefore (i)~voxel-hashed or precomputed neighbourhoods rather than
per-patch k-d tree queries, (ii)~a fused batched gather kernel, and
(iii)~CUDA-class hardware, on which the batched models have substantial
headroom.

\section*{5. Supervision Without Ground Truth}

Every learned denoiser above is trained on the noise labels emitted by the
corruption model itself --- information no deployed system possesses, and
which cannot be obtained from real data, since the same scene cannot be
recorded simultaneously with and without rain. Whether the approach survives
without those labels is therefore the decisive question for its practical
relevance, and it is tested directly here.

Following LIORNet [arXiv:2603.19936], per-point pseudo-labels are derived
from the physics of a weather return using only a single corrupted scan:
(i)~a \emph{range-conditioned intensity anomaly} --- received power falls as
$\rho/r^2$, so the median intensity is estimated per range bin from the scan
itself and a return is anomalous only relative to its own range, a global
threshold would merely relabel ``far'' as ``noise''; (ii)~\emph{neighbourhood
range coherence} --- a real surface is continuous in the range image whereas
a droplet floats in front of what it occluded; and (iii)~\emph{edge
protection} --- genuine silhouettes also produce discontinuities, but
coherent ones persisting across adjacent pixels. LIORNet's fourth rule uses
zero-reflectivity, which Ouster sensors report and the KITTI HDL-64E does
not, so it is omitted rather than approximated. The restoration head is
disabled in this configuration: its target is the clean range of a known
source point, which is ground truth by construction.

\begin{table}[h]
\centering\small
\begin{tabular}{lcccc}
\toprule
\textbf{Variant} & \textbf{CD (m)} & \textbf{HD$_{95}$} & \textbf{Removal F1} & \textbf{Reten.}\\
\midrule
Pseudo-label rules alone (no network) & 0.465 & 3.297 & 0.484 & 70.5\%\\
Self-supervised network & 0.436 & 2.564 & 0.491 & 67.7\%\\
Supervised network & \textbf{0.227} & \textbf{0.460} & \textbf{0.813} & 65.3\%\\
\bottomrule
\end{tabular}
\end{table}

The pseudo-labels themselves score precision 0.500 / recall 0.470 / F1 0.484
against the true labels: roughly half of what the self-supervised model is
told is wrong. Trained on them, the network reaches 60\% of the supervised
model's removal F1 --- but essentially all of that is supplied by the rules
rather than by learning, since the network moves F1 only from 0.484 to 0.491
($+1.4\%$ relative). It reproduced its supervision instead of generalising
past it, and reporting the ratio to the supervised model alone would credit
the network for what three physical heuristics already deliver.

Learning does help in one respect: HD$_{95}$ improves from 3.297 to 2.564
(22\%) and CD from 0.465 to 0.436, so the network is better than the rules
at catching the most damaging surviving outliers even though its average
classification accuracy is not.

\textbf{This is a negative result for self-supervision as implemented, and a
useful one:} the gap between F1 0.49 and 0.81 measures exactly what
knowledge of the ground truth is worth on this task, and no additional
training on these rules closes it. The prerequisite for progress is better
pseudo-labels --- multi-echo returns, temporal consistency across successive
sweeps, or the reflectivity channel this sensor does not provide --- not
longer training.

\section*{6. Experimental Validity}

\textbf{Protocol.} Every method is run on the \emph{identical} noisy cloud
for a given (frame, seed) pair, over three independent noise draws across
ten held-out frames. Results are therefore paired, and each ranking claim
is supported by a Wilcoxon signed-rank test over the 30 paired observations
(recorded in \texttt{results/metrics\_summary.json}); dispersion is reported
as a standard deviation alongside every mean. Reconstruction quality is
reported as Chamfer Distance and HD$_{95}$; strict Hausdorff is retained
only in the raw output, because as the maximum over all residual points it
is pinned near 30~m for every method by a single survivor at the range gate
and consequently ranks nothing. Because geometric error alone cannot
distinguish removing noise from displacing everything slightly, methods
that yield an index set into the noisy cloud (both filters, and the DGCNN
via its outlier head) additionally report noise-removal precision, recall
and F1 against the ground-truth injection labels.

\textbf{Corrections applied during review.} Six defects affecting reported
numbers were identified and fixed; they are recorded here because several
bear directly on the conclusions.

\begin{enumerate}\setlength{\itemsep}{2pt}
\item \emph{Autoencoder patch recombination.} The AE's unordered
reconstructions were concatenated across overlapping patches and
voxel-downsampled, returning 165\% of the input point count --- inflating
its Chamfer term from the denoised side and presenting the detector with a
cloud of the wrong density. Each input point now adopts its nearest
reconstructed point within its own patch, averaged across overlaps, giving
output cardinality equal to input and a like-for-like comparison with the
DGCNN.
\item \emph{Autoencoder training target.} The Chamfer target was the
nearest-clean-point projection of \emph{every} noisy point, outliers
included, so roughly 15\% of the target set consisted of surface points
duplicated wherever outliers happened to fall. The target is now a genuine
clean patch gathered about the patch centroid.
\item \emph{Temporal filter ego-motion.} Inter-sweep translation was assumed
negligible against the 0.5~m consistency radius. Measured by ICP over these
drives it averages 0.56~m and reaches 0.94~m (3--9~m/s), exceeding the
radius on half of all frames; uncompensated, the filter deletes static
structure rather than backscatter. Registration now precedes the
consistency test.
\item \emph{Patch coverage.} Randomly seeded patch centres left 2{,}147 of
36{,}033 points (6\%) outside every patch on a representative frame. Such
points retained an outlier probability of zero and could never be removed,
capping the DGCNN's removal recall independently of classifier quality.
Coverage is now completed greedily, at a cost of 27\% more patches
(+38~ms/frame) --- a latency regression accepted deliberately.
\item \emph{Filter timing.} SOR and ROR started their timers after the
NumPy-to-Open3D conversion while the learned methods were timed end-to-end,
flattering the baseline in the latency comparison. All methods are now
timed end-to-end from a NumPy array.
\item \emph{Degenerate frames.} An aggressive filter can empty a sparse
frame; the metrics returned infinity, which propagated silently into
averaged tables. Such frames now yield NaN, are excluded from means, and
are counted explicitly in the results table.
\end{enumerate}

Corrections (1) and (2) bear on the project's central claim that Chamfer
Distance is a poor proxy for downstream detection quality, since that claim
rested largely on the autoencoder's collapse and the autoencoder was
handicapped by two defects unrelated to its architecture. Section~3 reports
the claim as re-measured after the fixes.

\textbf{Remaining limitations.} The noise is synthetic: the LISA model is
a physically motivated approximation, not measured adverse-weather data,
and validation on a real corrupted dataset (e.g. STF or the Canadian
Adverse Driving Conditions set) remains the natural next step. The
detection-consistency protocol measures agreement with the detector's own
clean-cloud output rather than ground truth, so it bounds recoverable
performance without certifying absolute accuracy. The intensity channel is
zero-filled before detection. Finally, both learned models were trained for
10 epochs on laptop hardware; the DGCNN's validation loss was still
decreasing slowly at that point, so its results should be read as a floor
on what the architecture achieves rather than a converged ceiling.

\section*{7. Deliverables and Scope Notes}

\begin{sloppypar}
The repository contains the modular pipeline
(\texttt{src/lidar\_denoise/}), a YAML-configured driver
(\texttt{scripts/run\_pipeline.py}), the detection evaluation
(\texttt{scripts/detection\_eval.py}), an interactive before/after demo
(\texttt{scripts/demo.py}), and 23 unit tests covering preprocessing,
the weather-model physics, the filters, ego-motion registration, the
metrics and degenerate-input handling. All metrics and plots are written
under \texttt{results/}, alongside the corrected proposal source.
\end{sloppypar}

The YDLIDAR~X2 and Raspberry~Pi live-capture component of the proposal is
hardware-dependent and remains deferred; the pipeline is sensor-agnostic
and accepts any $(N,3)$ scan, so a driver for that sensor can replace the
KITTI reader without further changes.
